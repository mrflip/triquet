[quote="Q11"]11. Among the six seals (pictured) at the Texas State Capitol, the historical region blazoned «rampant gules» here joined in union with one neighbor («embattled Or») in 1230, then another in 1479 — [b]name any of those three regions[/b]...

Answer: [spoiler][b]León, Castile, or Aragon (Léon of [i]The Professional[/i]; Castile soap; Catherine of Aragon)[/b][/spoiler]
Correct Answer %:
This is one of my favorite questions and also the worst-written of them.

There's a delightful "programming language" for specifying heraldic coats of arms. It (Heraldic Blazonry: «Azure, three fleurs-de-lis Or», 1200ish CE), like Sanskrit grammar (Pāṇini's Ashtadhyayi, 500ish BCE), musical notation (🎶 1000ish CE), and chemical nomenclature (1,3,7-trimethyl-3,7-dihydro-1H-purine-2,6-dione†, 1900ish) gives an unambiguous, verifiable mapping from word/symbol to concrete essentials, without constraining richness of the product.

I love how much European history is tied up in those little symbols on the floor -- and so kept trying to pack it all into a question that couldn't work. Ultimately I downplayed the heraldry (which nobody but me liked) and made it a half-assed European History question. But this is the recap, so I get to geek out about the heraldry, and the scroll bar is available for your use.

"Six" in the question is to cue the "Six flags over Texas":

  [list]
  [*] [b]León[/b] alone is «Argent, a lion rampant Purpure crowned Or» -- A silver shield with a [b]purple[/b] lion standing on its hind legs, wearing a gold crown.
  [*] [b]Castile[/b] is «Gules, a castle Or» -- a red shield with a gold three-towered castle. Here, as Castile and León, we have «Quarterly, 1 and 4 Argent, a lion rampant Gules crowned Or; 2 and 3 Gules, a tower Or ajouré Azure» -- white quarters with crowned red lions, alternating with red quarters with gold towers, their windows and door filled with blue.[/list]
[*] [b]France[/b] (La Salle's colony, 1685–1689): «Azure, three fleurs-de-lis Or».
[*] [b]Mexico[/b] (1821–1836): «Azure, an eagle proper, wings elevated, perched upon a nopal Vert, grasping a serpent Argent».

[*] [b]CSA[/b] (1861–1865): «Azure, an equestrian figure of Washington Argent, within a wreath of cotton, tobacco, cane and grain Argent, tied Or».

The union of the crowns mentioned is 1479, thirteen years before the war to conquer Granada concluded. That left Ferdinand (C&L) and Isabella (Aragon) in a good enough to buy shares in a crackpot scheme to sail over the horizon, The pope drew a line, and Texas was part of Spain, except it wasn't. Their union was dynastic: a marriage, not a political merging. The "Spanish Indies" belonged to the Crown of Castile, not Spain, until at least the 1700s,.
